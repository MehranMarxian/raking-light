Documentation plates

One surface, four readings

Conservators photograph inscriptions and paintings under raking light to reveal tool marks that flat light hides. This app turns that technique into a medium. All four plates below are the same height field; only the lamp moves.

Under the hood

How the surface is solved

Pictures become cell targets

Each picture is reduced to 96 × 96 cells and paired with a lamp direction. Every cell is backed by a 4 × 4 patch of heights, so the surface has 147,456 heights to tune and room for small facets that face different lamps.

Gradient descent on the light

A renderer shades every facet with Lambert lighting, so facets turned away from a lamp fall dark. Brightness is averaged per cell, the way your eye averages it from a step back. Adam adjusts all heights together until each lamp's cells match its picture. A fourth term asks the overhead view to stay one even grey, which is why the plate reads as noise until the lamp drops.

Trade-off
Two lamps set 90° apart act independently, so their pictures separate cleanly. A third lamp has to share facets with the other two: a cell that is bright for one lamp tends to go dark for the others, which leaves faint negative ghosts.

The full app

What the app adds

Lamp layouts

Two to four lamps at any direction and height. While you drag a lamp, a quick preview solve shows how much ghosting the layout will cost.

Sun mode

Enter the wall's compass bearing and your city. Pictures are assigned to sun positions, so one appears in the morning and another only near sunset.

Print-ready export

Sizes in millimetres, a base and a hanging slot, and a slope limit matched to the printer so thin facets don't break in casting.

Photographic targets

Portrait mode reshapes contrast before solving, since faces need more midtones than graphic shapes do. Photogrammetry scans can also serve as the base form.
